Diagnosis

1 finding(s); most likely: CPU preemption: rtdemo[8536085] waits for a higher-priority thread (high confidence). Kernel trace (1001 ms): 1 of 1 finding(s) confirmed step by step.

Diagnosis 20261122-135354-diagnose-rtdemo, taken 2026-11-22T13:53:54 from 16 thread-state snapshots of the target.

CPU preemption: rtdemo[8536085] waits for a higher-priority thread

high confidence

rtdemo[8536085] 'A-control' (priority 15) is ready to run but rtdemo[8536085] 'B-worker' (priority 20) holds its CPU. The scheduler always runs the highest-priority ready thread, so the deadline task waits until the CPU-heavy thread stops. No lock is involved.

Evidence

  • rtdemo[8536085] 'A-control' (priority 15) was READY (able to run, not running) in 5 of the 16 snapshots it appeared in.
  • In 5 of those, a thread with a higher priority was RUNNING on its CPU (CPU 1): mostly rtdemo[8536085] 'B-worker' at priority 20 (5 time(s)).
  • This was the usual situation, not a one-off: the higher-priority thread was running in 5 of 5 READY snapshots.
  • The deadline report for A-control shows 330 misses in 110 run(s) of consecutive misses; 110 of those runs started with waiting for the CPU (late start or preempted) and 0 with a lock wait. Worst wait for the CPU 35.1 ms; worst lock wait 0.0 ms.
  • Caution: the 99th-percentile wait for the CPU (34.8 ms; worst 35.1 ms) is within twice the measured timer lateness of CPU 1 (28.4 ms); part of it may be virtual-machine noise.

Could not determine

  • Snapshots are instants: 16 were taken. They show the higher-priority thread had the CPU at those moments, not for how long overall.
  • Kernel threads (procnto) are not counted as the cause; their CPU use is not analysed.
  • Whether the higher-priority thread's work is legitimate: the rule sees priorities and states, not what the threads are for.
  • A worst case: finite runs cannot prove the deadline will always be met after a fix.

Suggested fix: Run rtdemo[8536085] 'A-control' at a higher priority than rtdemo[8536085] 'B-worker' (now 15 vs 20), or lower the CPU-heavy thread's priority. On QNX a higher number is a higher priority and a FIFO thread keeps the CPU until it blocks, so a deadline-critical thread must sit above CPU-heavy work on its CPU. This is a scheduling-parameter change; no lock or code change is involved.

Kernel trace check

Confirmed by kernel trace: every step was seen.

  • Seen: rtdemo[8536085] 'A-control' is ready to run but not running, and not waiting on a lock. READY 334.4 ms in 71 span(s); MUTEX 0.0 ms in a 1001 ms trace
  • Seen: While it waits, rtdemo[8536085] 'B-worker' runs on the same CPU. rtdemo[8536085] 'B-worker' was RUNNING on the waiting thread's CPU for 319.2 ms of its 334.4 ms READY time (95%)
  • Seen: rtdemo[8536085] 'B-worker' runs at a higher priority than the waiting thread. 319.2 of those 319.2 ms were at a priority above the waiting thread's (priorities as recorded in the THREAD events)
  • The trace covers 1001 ms of the run; it shows what happened in that window only.

Limitations

  • These are finite runs (3 before, 3 after). They show the effect of the change on this workload; they do not prove a worst-case response time or that the deadline can never be missed.
  • The target is a QNX 8.0 virtual machine. Virtual-machine timing is noisier than real hardware, and absolute numbers vary between boots; only runs from the same session are compared.
  • Timing check of the target, CPU 0 (2026-11-22T13:04:24): 508 timer wake-ups at a 1 ms period in 10 s; mean lateness 19.08 ms, worst 63.1 ms, 488 wake-ups more than 1 ms late, with nothing else of ours running.
  • Timing check of the target, CPU 1 (2026-11-22T13:04:13): 4800 timer wake-ups at a 1 ms period in 10 s; mean lateness 1.31 ms, worst 28.4 ms, 4754 wake-ups more than 1 ms late, with nothing else of ours running.
  • The workload is a simulated, educational demo. Nothing here is a claim about any automotive, medical or other safety-certified system.
  • Cross-checks: qbridge's statistics agreed with the program's own summary in 6 of 6 runs, and with qbridged's message-passing statistics in 6 of 6 runs.
