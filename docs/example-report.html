Diagnosis

1 finding(s); most likely: Priority inversion on a mutex in rtdemo[663554] (high confidence).

Diagnosis 20261117-125600-diagnose-rtdemo, taken 2026-11-17T12:56:00 from 29 thread-state snapshots of the target.

Priority inversion on a mutex in rtdemo[663554]

high confidence

rtdemo[663554] 'A-control' (priority 30) waits for a mutex held by rtdemo[663554] 'C-logger' (priority 10), while rtdemo[663554] 'B-worker' (priority 20) keeps the CPU. The holder cannot finish its critical section until the middle-priority work stops, so the high-priority thread waits for unrelated work.

Evidence

  • In 8 of 29 snapshots rtdemo[663554] 'A-control' (priority 30) was blocked on the mutex at 0x4d57bb4050, for up to 33 ms.
  • The mutex holder was rtdemo[663554] 'C-logger' at priority 10 (read from the mutex's owner word in process memory) in 8 of those snapshots: below the waiter, so it was not running at the waiter's priority.
  • In 8 snapshot(s) a thread between the two priorities had the holder's CPU: rtdemo[663554] 'B-worker' at priority 20 (RUNNING).
  • The mutex's protocol flags say PTHREAD_PRIO_NONE (from its count word, flag _NTO_SYNC_PRIONONE)
  • The deadline report for A-control shows 242 misses in 76 run(s) of consecutive misses; 75 of those runs started with a long lock wait (worst lock wait 36.9 ms).

Could not determine

  • Snapshots are instants: 29 were taken. They show these states happened, not how often or for how long overall.
  • The holder is decoded from the mutex's owner word using a layout observed on QNX 8.0 (pid low bits and tid); it is not a documented interface. The pid bits matched in 8 of 8 reads.
  • Which source line created or locks this mutex: the rule sees addresses and threads, not code.
  • Whether other locks or resources also delay the waiter.
  • A worst case: finite runs cannot prove the deadline will always be met after a fix.

Suggested fix: Let the mutex use priority inheritance, the QNX default: create it with PTHREAD_PRIO_INHERIT (or without setting a protocol) instead of PTHREAD_PRIO_NONE. The holder then runs at the waiter's priority while the waiter is blocked, so a middle-priority thread can no longer delay it.

The change

Restore priority inheritance on the shared lock (QNX default), as suggested by the mutex priority-inversion diagnosis

Limitations

  • These are finite runs (3 before, 3 after). They show the effect of the change on this workload; they do not prove a worst-case response time or that the deadline can never be missed.
  • The target is a QNX 8.0 virtual machine. Virtual-machine timing is noisier than real hardware, and absolute numbers vary between boots; only runs from the same session are compared.
  • Timing check of the target, CPU 0 (2026-11-17T13:14:30): 627 timer wake-ups at a 1 ms period in 10 s; mean lateness 15.29 ms, worst 55.5 ms, 349 wake-ups more than 1 ms late, with nothing else of ours running.
  • Timing check of the target, CPU 1 (2026-11-17T13:14:20): 9993 timer wake-ups at a 1 ms period in 10 s; mean lateness 0.15 ms, worst 2.3 ms, 4 wake-ups more than 1 ms late, with nothing else of ours running.
  • The workload is a simulated, educational demo. Nothing here is a claim about any automotive, medical or other safety-certified system.
  • Cross-checks: qbridge's statistics agreed with the program's own summary in 6 of 6 runs, and with qbridged's message-passing statistics in 6 of 6 runs.
