Diagnosis

1 finding(s); most likely: Priority inversion on a condition variable: rtdemo[15847445] likely waits for P-producer (medium confidence). Kernel trace (1001 ms): 1 of 1 finding(s) confirmed step by step.

Diagnosis 20261123-201549-diagnose-rtdemo, taken 2026-11-23T20:15:49 from 17 thread-state snapshots of the target.

Priority inversion on a condition variable: rtdemo[15847445] likely waits for P-producer

medium confidence

rtdemo[15847445] 'A-control' waits on a condition variable. Nobody owns a condition variable, so QNX cannot raise the thread that will signal it to the waiter's priority. rtdemo[15847445] 'P-producer' (priority 10) was ready to run but rtdemo[15847445] 'B-worker' (priority 20) had its CPU; if it is the thread that signals, the waiter waits for the middle-priority thread. This is a priority inversion without a lock.

Evidence

  • rtdemo[15847445] 'A-control' (priority 30) was blocked on a condition variable in 13 of 17 snapshots (sync object 0x485a1ba018).
  • In 7 of those, rtdemo[15847445] 'P-producer' (priority 10), a thread of the same process below the waiter, was READY but not running, while rtdemo[15847445] 'B-worker' (priority 20) ran on its CPU (CPU 1).
  • The deadline report for A-control shows 678 misses in 110 run(s) of consecutive misses; 110 started with a long wait in the task's synchronisation step (worst 44.4 ms).

Could not determine

  • Which thread will signal the condition variable: it has no owner, so this is the likeliest candidate (same process, below the waiter, held off its CPU), not a proven one. A kernel trace (--trace) can show whether each wait ends right after it runs.
  • Snapshots are instants, not totals.
  • A worst case: finite runs cannot prove the deadline will always be met after a fix.

Suggested fix: There is no priority inheritance on a condition variable, and PTHREAD_PRIO_INHERIT on its mutex does not change that. Run the signalling thread above the middle-priority work (now 10 vs 20); or let the waiter do the work itself; or ask for it with MsgSend from a server, which on QNX runs at its client's priority.

The change

Run the producer above the CPU-heavy worker (10 -> 25), as the condition-variable diagnosis suggests: a condition variable has no priority inheritance, so the signalling thread's own priority must be above the middle-priority work. Same binary.

Limitations

  • These are finite runs (3 before, 3 after). They show the effect of the change on this workload; they do not prove a worst-case response time or that the deadline can never be missed.
  • The target is a QNX 8.0 virtual machine. Virtual-machine timing is noisier than real hardware, and absolute numbers vary between boots; only runs from the same session are compared.
  • Timing check of the target, CPU 0 (2026-11-22T14:57:53): 538 timer wake-ups at a 1 ms period in 10 s; mean lateness 18.17 ms, worst 55.3 ms, 350 wake-ups more than 1 ms late, with nothing else of ours running.
  • Timing check of the target, CPU 1 (2026-11-23T17:08:55): 9992 timer wake-ups at a 1 ms period in 10 s; mean lateness 0.13 ms, worst 1.9 ms, 7 wake-ups more than 1 ms late, with nothing else of ours running.
  • The workload is a simulated, educational demo. Nothing here is a claim about any automotive, medical or other safety-certified system.
  • Cross-checks: qbridge's statistics agreed with the program's own summary in 6 of 6 runs, and with qbridged's message-passing statistics in 6 of 6 runs.
